<h2>FAQ: Does Hard Water Damage Pipes</h2>

<h3>Does hard water damage pipes in Indian tank homes?</h3>
<p>It commonly builds scale that narrows fittings, shower branches and geyser paths. Buried PVC or PPR may look fine while chrome ends suffer. Older metal lines can hold more crust and need plumber assessment if flow collapses or leaks appear.</p>

<h3>Does hard water damage plumbing even if plastic pipes look fine?</h3>
<p>Yes, in the practical sense. Valves, aerators, connectors and hot branches can still clog and wear while plastic runs remain intact.</p>

<h3>What is hard water scale in pipes made of?</h3>
<p>Mostly calcium and magnesium minerals that settle when hard water is heated or left to evaporate and concentrate on surfaces.</p>

<h3>Are hard water deposits in pipes the same as rusty water?</h3>
<p>Not always. White chalk is classic hardness scale. Brown water often involves iron or corrosion and needs separate diagnosis.</p>

<h3>Does hard water affect pipes more on the hot side?</h3>
<p>Often yes. Heating encourages scale, so geyser branches and hot mixers show problems earlier than little-used cold taps.</p>

<h3>Will Scale-O clear old scale inside buried lines this week?</h3>
<p>No. Reset accessible fittings and call a plumber for serious blockages. Scale-O helps slow new deposits after correct install and sizing.</p>

<h3>Does Scale-O soften water so pipes cannot scale?</h3>
<p>No. It conditions minerals for scale behaviour. Hardness strip readings and soap lather usually stay similar.</p>
