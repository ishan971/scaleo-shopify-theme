<h2>What Water Hardness Actually Measures</h2>
<p>Hard water is water with a lot of dissolved calcium and magnesium. These minerals come from rock and soil as groundwater moves through them. When hard water is heated or left to dry, the minerals come out as white scale on taps, tiles, shower heads and geyser elements.</p>
<p>Hardness is usually reported as <strong>mg/L as CaCO3</strong> (calcium carbonate). For water, 1 mg/L is practically the same as 1 ppm, so you will see both units used for the same number. Some foreign test kits use other units:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>gpg (grains per gallon):</strong> 1 gpg is about 17.1 ppm. To convert gpg to ppm, multiply by 17.1.</li>
<li><strong>°dH (German degrees):</strong> 1 °dH is about 17.8 ppm.</li>
<li><strong>°f (French degrees):</strong> 1 °f is 10 ppm.</li>
</ul>
<p>So if an imported strip says "10 gpg", your water is about 171 ppm, which is hard. The <a href="https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USGS guide to water hardness</a> explains the units and the scale used in this article.</p>

<h2>TDS Level for Hard Water: Why a TDS Meter Is Not a Hardness Test</h2>
<p>The most common reading Indian families have is from a TDS pen, often shown by an RO technician. TDS (total dissolved solids) counts <strong>everything</strong> dissolved in the water: calcium and magnesium, but also sodium, chloride, sulphate, bicarbonate and more. Hardness counts only calcium and magnesium.</p>
<p>That means:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>High TDS does not always mean very hard.</strong> Coastal borewell water can have high TDS from salt (sodium chloride) with only moderate hardness.</li>
<li><strong>Moderate TDS can still be hard.</strong> Water from limestone areas can be mostly calcium, so even a middle TDS reading produces a lot of scale.</li>
<li><strong>There is no reliable formula</strong> to convert TDS into hardness. The ratio changes from one source to another.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use TDS as a rough clue. If your TDS is above about 300 to 500 ppm and you also see white scale on taps, your water is very likely hard. To get a real hardness number, use hardness test strips, a drop-count (titration) kit, or a lab report. For the full explanation, read <a href="/blogs/articles/difference-between-scaling-tds-and-hardness">the difference between scaling, TDS and hardness</a>.</p>

<h2>Borewell Water Hardness Level: What Is Normal in India</h2>
<p>Borewell water is groundwater, and its hardness depends on the rock it passes through. In many hard-rock and dry regions of India, borewell hardness is often above 200 mg/L, and readings of 300 to 600 mg/L are not unusual. Some pockets go higher. The <a href="https://cgwb.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Central Ground Water Board (CGWB)</a> publishes groundwater quality reports that show how hardness, salinity and other parameters vary by state and district.</p>
<p>Three things make borewell readings tricky:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>They change with season.</strong> Readings often rise in summer when water tables drop and fall slightly after the monsoon.</li>
<li><strong>They change with depth.</strong> A deepened borewell can give very different water from the old one.</li>
<li><strong>They vary street to street.</strong> Your neighbour's reading is a clue, not your answer.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Borewell water hardness limit:</strong> there is no separate limit for borewells. The same BIS drinking water limits apply (200 mg/L acceptable, 600 mg/L permissible). For scale in your home, anything above about 180 ppm will be very noticeable. If your borewell reads very high, also check for iron (yellow or orange stains) and get a lab report. Read more in <a href="/blogs/articles/water-softener-for-borewell">water softener for borewell water</a>.</p>

<h3>Tanker and mixed supply readings</h3>
<p>Tanker water is usually groundwater from somewhere else, so its hardness depends on the source and can change from one load to the next. Many independent houses mix municipal water, borewell water and tanker water in the same overhead tank. In that case, your tank reading moves week to week. Test the tank water itself, not just one source, and retest after a heavy tanker month.</p>

<h2>Water Hardness Level for Bathing</h2>
<p>There is no official "bathing" limit for hardness in India. In practice, families start to notice changes at these levels:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Under about 120 ppm:</strong> most people notice little difference in hair or skin feel.</li>
<li><strong>About 120 to 180 ppm:</strong> soap and shampoo lather less, and some people notice a film on hair.</li>
<li><strong>Above about 180 ppm:</strong> coated hair, a tight or dry skin feel after bathing, and soap residue in the bathroom become common complaints.</li>
</ul>
<p>Hot water from the geyser makes this more noticeable in winter. These are comfort effects, not medical conditions. If you have ongoing hair fall, scalp or skin problems, see a doctor or dermatologist. Hard water can make hair and skin feel worse; treating water is not a medical treatment.</p>

<h2>Honest Notes for Very High Readings</h2>
<p>If your reading is very high, here is what you should know before buying any product, including ours.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Above about 600 ppm hardness:</strong> get a lab report. Scale-O can still help slow new scale in the tank, but expect results to take longer, and keep cleaning fittings more often. For very severe water, some homes use more than one stage of treatment.</li>
<li><strong>Very high TDS (above about 1,500 to 2,000 ppm), salty taste:</strong> this is usually salinity, not just hardness. A conditioner works on scale behaviour; it does not reduce TDS or salt. Use RO for drinking and get local advice for the house line.</li>
<li><strong>Yellow, orange or brown stains:</strong> that is usually iron. Scale-O is not an iron filter. Treat iron separately.</li>
<li><strong>Smell, colour or cloudiness:</strong> may point to contamination. Get a lab test before anything else.</li>
</ul>
<p>Being clear about these limits helps you buy the right thing the first time. If you are unsure, share your test reading with Scale-O support before you order.</p>

<h2>Will a Water Softener Change My Hardness Reading?</h2>
<p>It depends on the type.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Salt-based (ion-exchange) softener:</strong> yes. It removes calcium and magnesium, so a hardness strip will read soft after the plant.</li>
<li><strong>Salt-free conditioner like Scale-O:</strong> no. The minerals stay in the water but are conditioned so they are less likely to form hard scale. A hardness strip will read about the same. Judge Scale-O by what you see on taps, shower heads and the geyser, not by the strip.</li>
<li><strong>RO purifier:</strong> lowers hardness and TDS, but only at the one kitchen tap it serves.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you need the strip to read soft for a specific reason, a salt-based plant is the honest choice. If you want less scale across an overhead tank home without salt, power or plumbing, a tank insert fits better. Compare both in <a href="/blogs/articles/salt-free-vs-salt-based-water-softeners">salt-free vs salt-based water softeners</a>.</p>

<h2>FAQ: Water Hardness Levels</h2>

<h3>What is a normal water hardness level?</h3>
<p>Soft water is 0 to 60 ppm and moderately hard is 61 to 120 ppm. India's BIS drinking water standard sets 200 mg/L as the acceptable limit for total hardness. Many Indian borewell supplies read higher.</p>

<h3>What is the hard water ppm range?</h3>
<p>Water is usually called hard from 121 to 180 ppm and very hard above 180 ppm, measured as calcium carbonate.</p>

<h3>What TDS level means hard water?</h3>
<p>There is no exact TDS number for hard water, because TDS includes other salts too. As a rough clue, TDS above about 300 to 500 ppm with visible white scale usually means hard water. Use a hardness test to confirm.</p>

<h3>What is a normal borewell water hardness level?</h3>
<p>It varies widely by region. Many Indian borewells read above 200 mg/L, and 300 to 600 mg/L is common in hard-rock and dry areas. Test your own tank water.</p>

<h3>What water hardness level is good for bathing?</h3>
<p>There is no official limit. Most people notice little difference below about 120 ppm. Above about 180 ppm, coated hair and dry-feeling skin become common complaints.</p>

<h3>Will Scale-O lower my hardness reading?</h3>
<p>No. Scale-O conditions the minerals so they are less likely to form scale, but they stay in the water. Judge it by fittings and the geyser, not by a strip.</p>

<h3>Which Scale-O do I need for very hard water?</h3>
<p>Choose by tank size: Mini for 500 to 750 litres, Semi for 1000 to 1500 litres, Centi for 2000 to 2500 litres. For very hard water and a fast-emptying tank, size up if you are between two sizes.</p>

<h3>Is Scale-O suitable for apartments?</h3>
<p>Only if you have a private overhead tank you can open safely. Shared society tanks need a building-level solution.</p>
