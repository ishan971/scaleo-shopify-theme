<h2>Why Hard Water Laundry Feels Stiff and Dull</h2>
<p>Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. The <a href="https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">USGS hardness overview</a> explains why those minerals matter in household water. In an Indian independent house, that water often sits in an overhead tank, then runs to the washing machine inlet. During the wash, hardness minerals compete with detergent. Part of the detergent binds to minerals instead of lifting soil. Leftover mineral and detergent residue can stay in cotton and synthetics after the rinse.</p>
<p>That residue is why towels stiff after washing feel board-like even when the cycle looks normal on the display. It is why whites look grey instead of bright. It is why coloured clothes lose freshness faster than the label suggests. Families often respond by pouring more hard water detergent into every load. Extra detergent can worsen residue if the rinse water is still hard. More foam in the drum is not proof of cleaner fabric when minerals are the bottleneck.</p>
<p>Hard water laundry is therefore both a chemistry problem and a habit problem. The chemistry is minerals plus detergent. The habit is overdosing, short rinses, and judging results by scent instead of hand feel. Fix the water behaviour at tank level when you can. Then reset dose and rinse habits so fabric can show the difference.</p>

<h2>Hard Water Clothes: Towels, Whites, and Colours</h2>
<p>Towels are the loudest signal in many homes. Terry loops trap residue. A towel that used to dry skin quickly starts to push water around. That “stiff towel” complaint is classic hard water laundry, not always a bad dryer or a cheap brand.</p>
<p>Whites tell a slower story. Cotton shirts, bedsheets, and innerwear pick up a grey cast. People bleach more often. Bleach can help appearance once, then stress fibres if the mineral load never changes. Greying that returns within a few washes is a water clue, not only a detergent brand clue.</p>
<p>Colours dull when residue films the fibre surface and light reflects differently. Dark cotton can look dusty. Bright kidswear can look tired early. If the same detergent worked in a previous city with softer supply, and fails after a borewell or tanker season in your current house, treat water as a primary suspect.</p>
<p>School uniforms, gym wear, and kitchen cloths also show the pattern: they smell clean for an hour, then feel coated. That coated feel is the laundry version of bathroom mineral film.
